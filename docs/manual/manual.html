1. What Is the BSR AutoMotor System?

The BSR AutoMotor System is a smart, fully automated home water management solution. It exists to take away the daily chore of running your water motor by hand. Whether you are at home, at work or travelling, it watches your municipal (government / tap) water supply and your overhead or underground tank, and runs the pump automatically — exactly when it is needed, and never when it is not.

You set it up once, and the system takes care of the rest. It turns the motor ON when water is available or the tank is low, and OFF when the tank is full or the supply stops. On top of that you can see and control everything from your phone — either through the mobile app, or by simply opening the controller's own web page in a browser, with no app to install at all.

2. Host Controller — The Brain of the System

Installed at: near the water motor or the electrical panel.
Role: the Host Controller is the central unit. It reads the flow sensor, switches the motor, talks to the Sensor Node, keeps the clock, enforces every automation rule, manages your schedules, raises the alerts, serves the web control panel to your browser, and syncs with the cloud when internet is enabled.

2.1 Municipal Water Auto Start Improved

Purpose: automatically starts the motor the moment municipal (tap) water arrives in your supply pipe — so you never miss a drop of incoming water.

In most homes municipal water does not arrive at a fixed time. This feature watches the supply pipe continuously with a flow sensor. The instant water starts moving, the system recognises it and turns the motor ON to pump that water into your tank.

  • The flow sensor counts pulses from the pipe. Once the flow rate reaches the detection threshold — 0.5 litres per minute by default — the system confirms that municipal water has arrived.
  • As a second check, a minimum volume must be collected within a two-minute rolling window (100 ml by default). This prevents a brief pressure surge or a dripping tap from being mistaken for a real supply.
  • The motor starts automatically within seconds of detection. No action is needed from you.
  • A 3-minute minimum run time is enforced: the motor will not switch off for at least three minutes after starting. This stops false cut-outs caused by short fluctuations in flow.
  • After that, the motor stops automatically when flow falls below the cut-off threshold (0.4 litres per minute by default), confirming the supply has ended.
  • Every session records the total quantity of water pumped in litres, so you always know how much water each supply actually delivered.
  • Both thresholds and the confirmation volume are adjustable — see section 2.7.
  • Auto Start can be switched on or off at any time from the web panel or the mobile app.
  • The setting is saved permanently and is restored automatically after a power cut.
Note. Auto Start will not fire if you have already turned the motor on yourself with Direct Motor Control. This prevents the motor being double-triggered.
Note. If the motor is already running under Direct Control when municipal water arrives, the system still sounds the buzzer so you know water has come — but it leaves the motor state alone.

2.2 Tank Auto Fill — Automatic Level Control

Purpose: keeps your tank topped up automatically by watching its level and running the motor when it drops — so you never run out of water at home.

In this mode the controller works together with the Sensor Node mounted above your tank. The node measures the level every five seconds and reports it. Based on that reading, the controller decides whether the motor should run.

  • The motor turns ON automatically when the tank level falls to the start level or below — 50% by default.
  • The motor turns OFF automatically when the level reaches the stop level or above — 87% by default.
  • Both levels are adjustable from the web control panel (section 2.3).

The smart anti-chatter (hysteresis) system

Switching on and off at a single fixed level would make the motor flick on and off repeatedly as the water surface moves around that level. To prevent this, the system uses a layered decision instead:

Level readingDecisionReason
At or above the stop levelTank full → motor OFF The tank is full. There is nothing to pump.
Just below the stop level, when the tank was already marked full Still treated as full → motor stays OFF A small dead band that stops the motor restarting the instant it switched off.
Between the start and stop levels, motor not running Treated as full → motor stays OFF There is enough water. No action needed yet.
At or below the start levelTank not full → motor ON The tank is running low. Start refilling.
Below the stop level while the motor is already running Tank not full → motor stays ON Keep filling until it is genuinely full. Do not stop halfway.
Tip. This anti-chatter behaviour protects your motor from rapid repeated switching, which causes real wear and shortens motor life.
  • If the Sensor Node stops reporting for more than 2 minutes — for example a power cut at the tank — the controller treats the tank as full as a safety measure and stops the motor. A buzzer alert sounds immediately at the controller.
  • The web panel also shows how old the tank reading is, so a stale number can never be mistaken for a live one.
  • Tank Auto Fill is saved permanently and resumes automatically after a power cut.
Blocked. Tank Auto Fill cannot be switched on while the motor is already running under Municipal Water Auto Start or Direct Motor Control. This prevents two automations fighting over the same motor.

2.3 Adjustable Tank Start / Stop Levels New

Purpose: lets you decide how empty the tank must get before the motor starts, and how full it must be before the motor stops.
  • The start level (default 50%) is the point at which the tank counts as low. Raise it if you would rather the tank was always kept near full; lower it if you prefer fewer, longer runs.
  • The stop level (default 87%) is the point at which the tank counts as full.
  • The two levels must be at least five percentage points apart, so the automation always has a sensible band to work in.
  • Both are set from the Tank Levels card in the web control panel and are saved permanently in the device.
Why the stop level is capped at 87%. The ultrasonic probe sits above the full mark and, like all ultrasonic sensors, cannot measure anything closer than about 20–25 cm. Once the water rises past roughly 88%, the surface is inside the probe's blind zone and it stops returning a usable reading. A stop level set higher than that could never be reached — the motor would simply keep running until the tank overflowed. The system therefore refuses any value above 87%. See section 4.6 for how to raise this ceiling by remounting the probe higher.

2.4 Direct Motor Control

Purpose: gives you immediate manual control of the motor, overriding the automatic modes whenever you need to.

Sometimes you simply want the motor to run — to clean the tank, to test the installation, or to deal with something unusual. Direct Motor Control does exactly that, from the web panel or the mobile app.

  • Turn the motor ON or OFF instantly. Works on local Wi-Fi and remotely over the internet.
  • When you turn the motor ON manually, Tank Auto Fill is switched off automatically to avoid a conflict.
  • When you turn the motor OFF manually, all automatic flags are cleared — Auto Start, Tank Auto Fill and municipal water tracking all reset cleanly.
  • The motor state is saved permanently. If power fails while the motor is running, the motor resumes when power returns.
  • A short buzzer beep confirms every manual ON and OFF.
  • The system records that you started this run, so it shows up correctly in the run-time statistics and in the last-run record.
Blocked. A manual motor ON is refused if Municipal Water Auto Start has already detected water and started the motor, to prevent a double-trigger conflict. The panel tells you why.

2.5 Timer-Based Motor Scheduling

Purpose: runs the motor at a chosen time of day automatically — ideal where the municipal supply is predictable.
  • Up to five independent schedules can be active at once.
  • Each schedule sets a start time (hour, minute, second), a duration in minutes, and whether it repeats daily or runs just once.
  • If you set a time that has already passed today, the system automatically moves it to the same time tomorrow.
  • Repeating schedules roll forward by 24 hours after each run, so they keep going day after day with no further action from you.
  • One-time schedules are cleared from memory automatically once they have run.
  • All schedules survive a power cut.
  • Schedules can be viewed, edited and deleted from the web panel and the mobile app.
  • Deleting all schedules immediately stops any scheduled run that is in progress.
  • The panel also shows you when the next schedule is due (section 5.10).
Blocked. Schedules will not run until the clock has been set. The panel shows a "clock must be set first" reminder in this case.
Blocked. A schedule will not take over the motor while Municipal Water Auto Start or Tank Auto Fill is actively running it. It is simply re-evaluated on the next cycle.

2.6 Flow Sensor & Water Quantity Measurement

Purpose: measures exactly how many litres of municipal water each supply session delivers — so you have a real picture of what you receive.
  • A pulse-type water flow sensor is fitted to the pipe between the municipal supply and the motor.
  • Each pulse represents a precisely calibrated volume of water.
  • Pulses are counted continuously and converted into a live flow rate in litres per minute and a running total volume.
  • The total is displayed live on the web panel and in the mobile app, and is pushed to the cloud when internet is enabled.
  • The total resets to zero at the start of each new municipal supply session.
  • Totals are written to permanent memory, so a restart mid-session does not lose the measurement.

2.7 Adjustable Municipal Water Detection New

Purpose: lets you tune how sensitive the system is to incoming water, so it suits your own supply pressure.

Every supply line behaves differently. A high-pressure connection surges; a weak one trickles. Three settings on the web panel let you match the system to yours:

SettingDefaultWhat it does
Flow rate to detect water0.5 L/min How much flow counts as "water has arrived". Lower it if a weak supply is being missed; raise it if the system reacts to a trickle that is not a real supply.
Flow rate to cut motor off0.4 L/min How little flow counts as "the supply has stopped". Should always be set below the detection value, so the system does not oscillate between the two.
Volume to confirm supply100 ml How much water must actually be collected within a two-minute window before the system accepts that a genuine supply is running.
Tip. Leave these at their defaults unless you have an actual problem. If the motor sometimes starts when no real water has arrived, raise the detection rate or the confirmation volume. If a genuine supply is being missed, lower the detection rate.

2.8 Municipal Water Session Log New

Purpose: keeps a record of your recent municipal water supplies, so you can see when water came and how much you got.
  • The last ten supply sessions are stored in the device, newest first.
  • Each entry shows the date and time water arrived, the time it stopped, how long the supply lasted, and the litres collected.
  • A session that is still running is marked "still flowing" and its duration and volume update live.
  • Very short bursts are deliberately ignored, so a pipe simply pressurising does not clutter the log with meaningless one-second entries.
  • An in-progress session is checkpointed to permanent memory regularly, so a power cut costs at most a few minutes of the record rather than the whole session.
  • The log can be downloaded as a text file (section 2.20).

2.9 Motor Run-Time Statistics New

Purpose: tells you exactly how long your motor has been running, day by day, and what caused each run.
  • Run time is totalled for every day of the last 31 days and kept in the device.
  • Totals can be viewed for today, the last 7 days or the last 30 days.
  • Every total is split by what started the motor: mobile app, web page, schedule, municipal water, tank auto-fill, or the physical switch.
  • A daily bar chart shows the pattern at a glance; tapping any bar breaks that single day down by source.
  • Days are split at your local midnight, so the figures line up with real calendar days.
  • A run in progress is included in the totals as it happens.
Why this matters. Run time is the single best indicator of both your electricity use and your motor's wear. A month where "Tank auto-fill" suddenly dominates usually means a leak or a tap left open somewhere.

2.10 Last Run & Next Run Record New

Purpose: answers the two questions people actually ask — "why did the motor run?" and "when will it run next?"
  • Last started by — which of the six sources started the most recent run.
  • Last started at — the exact date and time it began.
  • Next schedule — when the next stored schedule is due to fire, including repeating schedules rolled forward to tomorrow.
  • All three survive power cuts.

2.11 Smart Buzzer Alert System

Purpose: keeps whoever is near the motor informed about important events, without needing to look at a phone.

The Host Controller has a built-in buzzer that uses different sound patterns for different events. This is genuinely useful when you are near the motor room and want to know what the system is doing without opening anything.

PatternSoundTriggered byMeaning
ContinuousOne long steady beep Municipal water arrived in Tank mode; Sensor Node disconnected; schedule completed; schedules deleted; device start-up Something important happened. Check the panel or the app.
Fast beepRapid short beeps Motor started by Tank Auto Fill The motor is starting because the tank is low.
Beep-beep-pauseTwo beeps, pause, repeat Wi-Fi connection lost; weak Wi-Fi signal detected Warning — a network or internet problem.
Medium alternatingEvenly paced on / off beeps Municipal water detected by Auto Start; direct motor ON / OFF command The motor has just been turned on or off.
Silent—System idle, everything normal All is well. No action needed.
  • Each alert has a maximum duration — around a second for simple confirmations, and up to a few minutes for a water-arrival alert that is meant to wake you.
  • Buzzer mute. The controller's buzzer can be switched off entirely from the Device Buzzer tile on the web panel. While muted, no alert will sound. The setting is saved and survives a power cut, so remember to switch it back on.
  • Stop Buzzer silences an alert that is sounding right now without disabling future alerts.
  • Special case: if the motor is already running under Direct Control when municipal water arrives, the buzzer still sounds to notify you, even though the motor state does not change.

2.12 Silencing the Tank Buzzer from Anywhere New

Purpose: lets you acknowledge the buzzer on the roof from your phone, instead of climbing up to it.

The Sensor Node has its own buzzer, out at the tank, where nobody can conveniently reach it. It sounds whenever the tank level leaves the safe band. You can now silence it from the control panel:

  • While the tank buzzer is actually sounding, a red banner appears at the top of the web panel carrying a single Stop Buzzer button.
  • The same command is always available as Stop Tank Buzzer under Controls.
  • The buzzer goes quiet within about five seconds of the tap.
  • The tank buzzer and the controller's own buzzer are silenced separately, so quieting one never hides an alert from the other.
This is an acknowledgement, not a permanent setting. As soon as the tank level returns to the normal range, the silence is cleared automatically and the buzzer re-arms itself, ready for the next alarm. A mute that outlived its alarm would leave the roof buzzer disarmed for good after one press — and nobody would notice until the tank overflowed. As a second safeguard, the Sensor Node re-arms its own buzzer if the controller stops confirming the silence for ten minutes: an alarm nobody can hear is worse than one nobody can silence.

2.13 Built-in Real-Time Clock

Purpose: keeps accurate date and time inside the device so schedules always run at the right moment — even after a power cut.
  • A dedicated hardware clock module with its own backup battery keeps time running while the mains power is off.
  • The clock drives the schedule system and date-stamps every record the device keeps.
  • The same module measures the ambient temperature to within a quarter of a degree, shown on the panel and in the app.
  • When internet is enabled, the clock synchronises itself with global internet time servers automatically.
  • You can also set it by hand from the web panel or the mobile app — useful on first setup or if it ever drifts.

2.14 Internet & Cloud Connectivity

Purpose: connects your system to the internet so you can monitor and control it from anywhere in the world.

When Remote / IoT mode is on, the Host Controller joins your home Wi-Fi router and communicates securely with a cloud service that relays commands and status between the device and your mobile app.

  • Live status is uploaded every few seconds: motor state, tank level, water flow status, temperature, total water pumped and more.
  • Commands from the app — motor on, motor off, set a schedule, change a mode — are delivered to the device and carried out in real time.
  • Works whether your phone is on your home Wi-Fi or on mobile data anywhere in the world.
  • All communication is authenticated. Only your own authorised account can send commands to your device.
  • Remote mode can be turned on or off at any time. With it off, the system runs entirely on its own local Wi-Fi and every automation continues exactly as normal.
Privacy and security. All connection credentials are held securely inside the device and are never shown to users or exposed in the app or the web panel.

2.15 Smart Wi-Fi Management

Purpose: keeps the system connected and recovers from network problems on its own, with no manual intervention.
  • The Host Controller always runs its own local Wi-Fi network, so the Sensor Node, the web panel and the mobile app have a working local connection even with no internet and no router.
  • When Remote mode is on, the controller also joins your home router. Both connections run at the same time.
  • Signal strength to the router is checked every few seconds. If it becomes too weak, a buzzer warning sounds and the system waits briefly for it to recover.
  • If it does not recover, the controller disconnects cleanly from the router and falls back to local-only operation.
  • If the router connection drops unexpectedly, reconnection is retried automatically for about a minute before falling back to local-only mode.
  • When the router connection is restored, the system restarts itself to re-establish the cloud link and re-sync the clock.
Tip. Even with no internet at all, every automation keeps working — Auto Start, Tank Auto Fill, schedules, alerts, and the complete web control panel. Internet is only needed for remote access from outside your home.

2.17 Two Firmware Copies & One-Tap Rollback New

Purpose: makes updating safe. If a new version ever misbehaves, the previous one is still in the device and can be restored instantly.
  • The device holds two firmware copies. An update is always written into the spare copy, so the version you were running stays intact.
  • After an update the device runs the new copy. If it fails to come up and check in correctly, it reverts to the previous copy automatically.
  • You can also roll back deliberately, at any time, with a single button on the web panel — no download and no internet required, because the older copy is already in the device.
  • The panel shows which copy is running now and which is held in reserve, each with its version number and build date.
  • Only one step back is kept. The next update overwrites the older copy.
  • A factory reset does not erase either firmware copy, so rollback still works afterwards.

2.18 Permanent Settings Memory (Power-Cut Safe)

Purpose: nothing you configure is ever lost when the power goes off.
  • Every setting is written to permanent memory inside the device the moment you change it.
  • After a power cut the device restores: Municipal Water Auto Start on/off; the motor state (if it was running, it resumes); Tank Auto Fill on/off; all five schedules; tank start and stop levels; water detection sensitivity; buzzer mute; Remote/IoT mode on/off; the water quantity counter; the water session log; run-time statistics; restart history; and the last-run record.
  • You never need to reconfigure anything after an outage. The system picks up exactly where it left off.

2.19 Health Monitoring & Restart History Improved

Purpose: records why the device restarted, so problems can be diagnosed quickly instead of guessed at.
  • Every restart is recorded with its date, time and cause — normal power on, a software update, a power brownout (a voltage dip), a hardware fault, and others.
  • The last sixteen restarts are kept, newest first, and shown on the web panel.
  • Lifetime totals per cause are kept separately, so you can see, for example, "Power on × 9, Brownout × 2" even after the detailed list has rolled over.
  • Health information is also uploaded to the cloud when internet is enabled, so the history can be reviewed from the app.
  • Device uptime — how long since the last restart — is shown on the panel at all times.
What to look for. Repeated Brownout entries mean the supply voltage at the device is dipping, usually when the motor starts. That is worth showing to an electrician. Repeated Power on entries simply mean mains outages.

Purpose: gives you control over the information the device has collected — to keep it, clear it, or start completely fresh.

3. Physical Controls on the Host Unit

Besides the web panel and the mobile app, the Host Controller has physical inputs on the unit itself. This lets an operator standing at the motor act immediately, with no phone in hand.

3.1 Tank Mode Rocker Switch

Purpose: lets the operator at the motor enable or disable automatic tank filling without opening anything.

A rocker switch is mounted on the Host Controller unit. It directly controls whether Tank Auto Fill is active.

Switch positionStateEffect
Pressed / closedTank mode ON Tank level monitoring starts. If the tank is at or below the start level, the motor turns on automatically.
Released / openTank mode OFF Tank level monitoring stops. If the motor was running because of tank mode, it stops immediately.
  • The switch is read every second, so a change takes effect at once.
  • Its setting is saved to permanent memory and is remembered after a power cut.
  • The state is mirrored on the web panel and in the app, so the switch and the software always agree.
  • A run started this way is recorded as started by the physical switch, so it shows up correctly in the run-time statistics.
Blocked. The rocker switch is ignored while the motor is already running under Municipal Water Auto Start or Direct Motor Control. It takes effect only in neutral conditions.

3.2 Flow Sensor (Automatic Input)

Purpose: detects the presence and rate of water in the supply pipe — the core sensor behind Municipal Water Auto Start.
  • The flow sensor is plumbed into the incoming water pipe and produces electrical pulses as water passes through it.
  • The system counts these pulses every second to calculate flow rate and to decide whether municipal water is present.
  • No manual action is ever needed — it works entirely automatically and feeds both the Auto Start logic and the litre measurement.

3.3 Temperature Sensor (Built In)

Purpose: provides an ambient temperature reading from inside the controller unit.
  • The clock module includes a temperature sensor accurate to about a quarter of a degree.
  • The reading is refreshed every second, shown live on the web panel and in the app, and uploaded to the cloud when internet is enabled.
  • It is a useful rough indicator of how hot your electrical panel or motor room is getting.

4. Sensor Node — The Tank Level Monitor

Installed at: above or beside the water tank, overhead or underground.
Role: a compact, independent unit that measures the water level in your tank and reports it to the Host Controller every five seconds. Without the Sensor Node, Tank Auto Fill cannot work.

4.1 Ultrasonic Water Level Sensing

Purpose: measures the level inside your tank without ever touching the water, using sound.

The Sensor Node uses a waterproof ultrasonic probe mounted above the tank. It sends a sound pulse downward and times the echo coming back off the water surface. That time gives the distance to the water, which is converted into a level percentage.

  • Tank depth and the probe's mounting height above the full mark are both configured to match your actual tank, and the offset is accounted for automatically.
  • Level % = (tank depth − distance down to the water surface) ÷ tank depth × 100
  • For a stable reading, five measurements are taken every cycle and the middle (median) value is used, which rejects noise and false reflections off the tank walls.
  • If no usable echo comes back — a blocked or disconnected probe, or heavy foam — the reading is flagged rather than reported as though it were real, and the previous figure is clearly marked as held over.
  • The web panel shows how old the reading is, so a stale value can never be mistaken for a live one.
Tip. The ultrasonic probe works with any water — clean, dirty or coloured — as long as the surface is in clear line of sight and nothing obstructs the space between the probe and the water.

4.2 Automatic Reporting to the Host Controller

Purpose: keeps the Host Controller continuously supplied with tank data so it can make real decisions about the motor.
  • Every 5 seconds the node measures the level and sends it to the Host Controller over the local Wi-Fi link.
  • Each report carries the level percentage, the raw distance reading, whether the reading is fresh or held over, and whether the tank is currently in an alarm condition.
  • If Wi-Fi drops, the node retries the connection by itself and resumes reporting as soon as it is back.
  • The onboard LED blinks briefly on each successful report — a quick visual confirmation that the unit is alive and talking.

4.3 Local Tank Buzzer Alerts

Purpose: gives an audible warning right at the tank when the level is critically low or nearly full.
ConditionBuzzerWhat it means
Level below 20%Short beep every second The tank is critically low. The motor should be running soon.
Level above 88%Long beep every second The tank is almost full. The motor should be stopping soon.
Level between 20% and 88%Silent Normal operating range. No alert needed.
Start-up completeTwo short beeps The Sensor Node has booted and connected to the Host Controller.
Who decides the alarm. The alarm band belongs to the Sensor Node, because it is a property of where that particular probe is mounted. The Host Controller reads the node's verdict rather than working it out again from its own numbers — which means there is only ever one set of limits, and it cannot drift out of step.

4.4 Remote Buzzer Silence

Purpose: lets you acknowledge the tank buzzer from the control panel instead of going up to the tank.

Full details are in section 2.12. In short: while the tank buzzer is sounding, a red banner appears at the top of the web panel with a single Stop Buzzer button, and the same command is always available as Stop Tank Buzzer under Controls. The buzzer goes quiet within about five seconds, and re-arms itself automatically once the tank level returns to normal.

4.5 Wi-Fi Communication & Firmware Update

Purpose: connects the Sensor Node to the Host Controller reliably over the longest possible distance, and allows its software to be updated wirelessly.
  • The node joins the Host Controller's own local Wi-Fi network automatically on start-up.
  • The link is deliberately configured for maximum range rather than maximum speed: full transmit power, the longest-reaching Wi-Fi mode, and power-saving sleep disabled so no report is ever missed. The node is usually on a roof, far from the controller.
  • If the connection drops it reconnects by itself, with no intervention.
  • LED indicator: lit = Wi-Fi disconnected or error; off = connected and working; quick blink = a reading was just sent successfully.
  • Firmware update: for the first 5 minutes after every restart, the Sensor Node accepts a wireless software update from a browser on the same network. After five minutes that window closes automatically and the node devotes itself entirely to measuring and reporting.
Why the update window closes. Keeping an update service running permanently on a device nobody can physically reach would be a standing risk. A short window that opens only when someone deliberately restarts the node gives easy maintenance without leaving that door open.

4.6 Mounting the Probe Correctly

Purpose: getting this right is the single biggest factor in how accurate your tank readings will be.

Every ultrasonic probe has a blind zone — a minimum distance below which it cannot measure at all. On the probe used here that is roughly 20–25 cm. If the probe is mounted only a little above the full mark, the water surface enters that blind zone while the tank is still filling, and the top of the tank is effectively unmeasurable.

Water levelDistance the probe seesResult
100% (full)≈ 12 cmInside the blind zone — no reading
94%≈ 17 cmInside the blind zone — no reading
88%≈ 22 cmMarginal
20%≈ 78 cmReliable
Recommendation for installers. Mount the probe at least 25 cm above the tank's full mark and set the mounting offset to match. Done that way, the whole usable range of the tank is genuinely measured and the stop level can be set higher with confidence. With a shorter offset, treat "full" as an inference rather than a measurement — which is precisely why the system caps the stop level at 87% by default.

Purpose: sets the two levels that drive Tank Auto Fill.
SettingDefaultMeaning

  • Save Tank Levels writes them permanently.
  • The two levels must be at least five percentage points apart.
If you try to set the stop level above 87%, the panel explains why it cannot: above roughly 88% the water surface is inside the ultrasonic probe's blind zone, so the motor would never see the tank reach that figure and would keep running until the tank overflowed. Section 4.6 explains how to raise this ceiling properly, by remounting the probe higher.

5.14 Municipal Water Detection

Purpose: tunes how sensitive the system is to incoming water.
  • Flow rate to detect water (default 0.5 L/min) — how much flow counts as water having arrived. Lower is more sensitive.
  • Flow rate to cut motor off (default 0.4 L/min) — how little flow counts as the supply having stopped. Keep this below the detection value.
  • Volume to confirm supply (default 100 ml) — how much water must actually be collected in a two-minute window before a supply is accepted as genuine.
  • Get From Device loads the values in use; Save Sensitivity stores your changes permanently.
Adjust only if you have a symptom. Motor starting when no real water has arrived → raise the detection rate or the confirmation volume. A genuine weak supply being missed → lower the detection rate.

5.15 Firmware Slots & Rollback

Purpose: shows which firmware is running, which is held in reserve, and lets you go back one step instantly.
  • Running now — the firmware the device is using at this moment, with its version number and the date it was built.
  • Previous / spare — the copy the last update replaced, still intact in the device.
  • Roll Back to Previous Firmware — restores that older copy on the next restart. No download and no internet are needed, because the copy is already there. You are asked to confirm first.
  • The button is disabled when there is no valid previous copy to go back to.
  • Only one step back is kept. The next update overwrites the older copy.
Automatic safety net. Immediately after an update, the device must start up and confirm it is healthy. If it cannot, it reverts to the previous copy by itself — you do not have to be there, and you do not have to notice.

7. Wi-Fi Extender — Bridging the Distance

Installed at: roughly midway between the Host Controller at the motor and the Sensor Node at the tank.
Role: repeats the Host Controller's own Wi-Fi network so the Sensor Node stays connected even when it is installed a long way away.

7.1 Range Extension

Purpose: solves the single most common installation problem — the tank being too far from the motor for a direct Wi-Fi link.
  • The Host Controller's local Wi-Fi network covers a limited range, typically 10–20 metres inside a building.
  • If your tank is on a rooftop while the motor is on the ground floor, the direct signal may be too weak to be reliable.
  • A standard household Wi-Fi range extender, placed at the midpoint, bridges the gap.
  • It repeats the controller's network under the same name, and the Sensor Node connects through it without knowing the difference. No configuration is needed in either device.
Tip. Any ordinary Wi-Fi repeater will do, configured to repeat the Host Controller's network (ESP32-WROOM). No special hardware is required. Place it where it still has a strong signal from the controller — a repeater in a weak spot simply repeats a weak signal.
How to tell whether you need one. Open the web panel and look at the tank level on the Live Status card. If it regularly says "updated 2 m ago" or worse, or drops to "no reading yet", the link is marginal and an extender will fix it.

8. Which Mode to Use & When

The system has four ways to start the motor. Understanding when each one earns its place is how you get the most out of the installation.

8.1 Choosing a Mode

Your situationRecommendedHow to enable it
Municipal water arrives at unpredictable times and you want the motor to run every time it does Municipal Water Auto Start Leave the Auto Start tile ON — it is the default. Nothing else to do.
You want the tank simply kept full, regardless of when water comes Tank Auto Fill Tap the Tank Auto Fill tile ON, or flip the rocker switch on the unit.
You need the motor running right now — tank cleaning, testing, an emergency Direct Motor Control Tap the power button on the motor card. Tap it again when you are done.
Municipal water arrives at the same time every day Schedules Set a repeating schedule with the start time and duration.
You are going away and want everything handled without you Auto Start + Tank Auto Fill together Turn both tiles ON. Between them they cover every scenario.

8.2 Mode Priority & Conflict Rules

The system has built-in rules so that two modes can never fight over the same motor. Every refusal comes with a plain-language explanation on screen.

SituationWhat happensWhy
Auto Start has already started the motor on incoming water — you tap Motor ON Blocked, with a message The motor is already running because of Auto Start. Starting it again would double-trigger it.
Motor is on via Auto Start — you try to enable Tank Auto Fill Blocked Tank control cannot take over while a municipal water session is running the motor.
Motor is on via Direct Control — municipal water arrives Motor stays on; buzzer sounds for a few minutes No conflict — the motor is already doing the right thing. The alert simply tells you water has also come.
Motor is on via Direct Control — you try to enable Tank Auto Fill Blocked Automatic control should not take over while you have deliberately taken manual control.
A schedule is due — but Auto Start or Tank Auto Fill is already running the motor The schedule does not activate The live session takes priority. The schedule is simply re-evaluated on the next cycle.
Tank Auto Fill is running — the Sensor Node stops reporting for 2 minutes Motor OFF; buzzer alert; tank assumed full A safety measure. Never run the motor without tank data — it could overflow the tank.
You set a schedule but the clock has never been set Schedule refused, with a "set the clock" message Schedules depend on real time. An unset clock would run the motor at the wrong hour.
You turn the motor OFF manually Everything clears Manual off is absolute: Auto Start, Tank Auto Fill and municipal tracking all reset cleanly, so nothing restarts behind your back.

8.3 Three Worked Examples

A. A typical city home with an unpredictable supply

  • Municipal Water Auto Start: ON — catch every supply automatically.
  • Tank Auto Fill: ON, start 50%, stop 87% — top up from the underground sump on the days no municipal water comes.
  • Schedules: none needed.
  • Check the Municipal Water sessions list after a fortnight to learn your real supply pattern.

B. A supply that arrives at the same time every day

  • Watch the sessions list for a week to find the real arrival time and typical duration.
  • Set schedule #1 to that time, with a duration slightly longer than a typical session, and Repeat ticked.
  • Keep Auto Start ON as well — it will catch any day the supply comes early or late.
  • Use Last & Next Run to confirm the next fire time is what you expected.

C. Away from home for two weeks

  • Turn Remote / IoT mode ON before you leave, and confirm the app connects from mobile data while you are still at home.
  • Auto Start ON and Tank Auto Fill ON.
  • Consider muting the Device Buzzer if the unit is near a neighbour — but remember to un-mute it when you return.
  • On your return, check Motor Run Time over 30 days. An unusual amount of "Tank auto-fill" while the house was empty points to a leak.

Motor restarted by itself after a power cutWorking as designed. The motor state is restored deliberately, so an interrupted fill continues.
Run time looks far too highUsually a leak or a tap left open. Look at which source is climbing. "Tank auto-fill" rising means the tank is emptying faster than it should.

12. Quick Start — Five Steps

From an installed system to full automation, in about ten minutes.

Step 1 — Install the Hardware

  • Mount the Host Controller near the motor or the electrical panel and connect the motor through it.
  • Fit the flow sensor in the pipe between the municipal supply and the motor, the right way round.
  • Mount the Sensor Node above the tank, pointing straight down, ideally at least 25 cm above the full mark (section 4.6).
  • If the tank is far from the motor, place a Wi-Fi extender midway between them.
  • Power everything up. The controller gives one long beep; the Sensor Node gives two short ones once it has connected.

Step 2 — Connect Your Phone

  • In Wi-Fi settings, join the network named ESP32-WROOM using the password supplied with your installation.
  • Open a browser and go to http://192.168.4.1
  • Tap the notice at the top once, to allow the page to play the water-arrival alert sound.
  • Confirm the header shows Device Connected.

Step 3 — Set the Clock

  • Open Controls and tap Config Clock.
  • Enter the current date and time and confirm.
  • Alternatively, turn on Remote / IoT mode (step 5) and let the device sync from the internet automatically.
Schedules will not be accepted until this is done.

Step 4 — Choose Your Mode

  • For most homes, leave Municipal Water Auto Start ON and turn Tank Auto Fill ON as well.
  • Open Tank Levels, tap Get From Device, and adjust the start and stop levels if the defaults of 50% and 87% do not suit your tank.
  • If your supply arrives at a fixed time, add a repeating schedule under Schedules and tap Save All.

Step 5 — Enable Internet (Optional but Recommended)

  • Open Remote / IoT Mode and switch it on. The panel may drop for a few seconds while the device changes Wi-Fi mode — this is normal.
  • Install the mobile app and sign in.
  • Test remote access before you need it: turn Wi-Fi off on your phone, and confirm the app still reads your device over mobile data.

Step 6 — After a Week, Look at the Data

  • Municipal Water sessions tells you when your supply really arrives and how much it delivers.
  • Motor Run Time over 7 days tells you what is actually driving your motor.
  • Use both to fine-tune your schedules and your tank levels. That is where a good installation becomes a very good one.

13.2 Routine Care

  • Every month — glance at Motor Run Time. A change in the pattern is usually the first sign of a leak or a failing float.
  • Every three months — check the ultrasonic probe is still clean, level and unobstructed. Dust, cobwebs and nests all affect readings.
  • Every three months — check the Reset History for brownouts.
  • Every six months — have the flow sensor inspected for scale or debris.
  • Before travelling — confirm remote access works over mobile data while you are still at home.
  • When a firmware update is offered — install it. Updates carry both fixes and new features, and rollback is always available if anything looks wrong.

13.3 Safety

Electrical work must be done by a qualified electrician. The Host Controller switches mains-powered motor equipment. Installation, wiring and any later change to the motor circuit are jobs for a licensed professional, in line with local regulations.
  • Always isolate the supply at the mains before opening any enclosure or working on the motor.
  • The motor can start without warning. That is the entire point of the product. Never assume a stopped motor will stay stopped — isolate the supply before any mechanical work on the pump, the pipework or the tank.
  • Keep the enclosure closed and dry. The Host Controller is not designed for direct exposure to water or weather.
  • Protect against dry running. This system controls when the motor runs, but it is not a substitute for the dry-run protection your pump itself should have.
  • Mount the Sensor Node securely. A probe that falls into the tank stops measuring and contaminates your water.
  • Do not modify the device or install firmware from any source other than BSR.
  • Supervise tank access. Open water tanks are a drowning hazard, particularly to children.

13.4 What the System Does Not Do

Stated plainly, so expectations are right from day one:

  • It does not measure water quality — only quantity and level.
  • It cannot measure the very top of the tank unless the probe is mounted high enough (section 4.6).
  • It cannot create water pressure. If your supply is weak, the system will detect and report that, but it cannot fix it.
  • It does not replace your pump's own overload or dry-run protection.
  • It will not run the motor during a mains power cut. When power returns, it restores exactly the state it was in.

14. Glossary
